# Internal Talent Optimization & Workforce Productivity Analytics
**Tools Used:** Microsoft Excel (Pivot Tables, Salary Cost Tiers, Performance Analysis, Workforce Metrics)

## 💡 1. The Business Problem & Objectives
*   **Context:** This project evaluates a comprehensive 1,000-row employee dataset tracking corporate staff members across 8 major departments over a 10-year operational period. On average, each department maintains around 125 employees.
*   **Problem Statement:** While the overall corporate performance rating stands at a solid mid-level baseline of 3.78 out of 5, organization leadership requires clear, data-backed insights to optimize compensation structures and maximize workforce productivity.
*   **Objectives:** 
    1. Identify salary disparities across specialized departments versus high-headcount operations.
    2. Map out the relationship between Training Hours investments and Performance Ratings to optimize future HR budgets.
    3. Evaluate departmental attendance consistency to establish corporate operational baselines.

## 🔍 2. Data Engineering & Cleaning
*   **Headcount Ingestion:** Grouped and summarized the raw 1,000-row employee table into discrete departmental blocks to accurately track staff density and organizational layout.
*   **Metric Isolation:** Isolated top-tier contributors to evaluate talent distribution, finding that 194 employees achieved a perfect performance rating of 5 (representing 19.4% of total staff).
*   **Data Alignment:** Format-locked the data layers to ensure numerical totals perfectly match across salary tables, training hour matrices, and performance distributions.

*   **💰 Salary Structures & Headcount Distribution:** The IT department records the highest average monthly salary (₱72,441.15), while Marketing has the lowest (₱65,619.96). The highest-paying departments (IT and Finance) maintain the fewest total employees (122 and 114 respectively), implying their work may be more complex or specialized. Conversely, lower-paying departments like Marketing, Sales, and Operations maintain steady employee counts, suggesting competitive engagement despite lower pay.
*   **⏱ Training and Performance ROI:** A clear relationship appears between Training Hours and Performance Ratings, suggesting that organizational training directly supports higher workplace performance. This is especially true for the HR department (5,255 training hours) and Customer Support (4,823 training hours), which both rank exceptionally high in both collective training and performance delivery.
*   **📈 Attendance Stability Baseline:** There is no significant correlation between attendance rates and departmental salary differences. All departments maintain a highly consistent attendance rate averaging around 90%, proving that overall workforce showing is stable regardless of pay variances.

## 🚀 4. Strategic Recommendations
1. **Increase Training Investment:** Because performance ratings rise concurrently with more training hours, the company should enhance both training quality and available hours. Focus this rollout especially in departments currently sitting below the corporate performance averages.
2. **Reassess Department Salary Structures:** Review and adjust incentive structures for Marketing, Sales, and Operations. Implementing targeted motivation and performance incentives will better align employee drive with overall pay equity.
3. **Maintain High Standards in IT and Finance:** These departments currently deliver strong pay-to-performance alignment. Retain this highly specialized talent pool through continuous upskilling programs and structural recognition.
4. **Encourage Consistent Attendance:** While attendance is already stable across the board, continued focus on reducing unexcused absences will indirectly support higher operational productivity.
